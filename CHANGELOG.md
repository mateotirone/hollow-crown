# Changelog

What changed in each version of The Hollow Crown, newest first. Written for players: say what they will notice, not which files moved.

How versions work is in [`docs/RELEASING.md`](docs/RELEASING.md).

## Unreleased

Everything merged to `main` since the last release goes here. Each pull request that changes what players see adds a line under one of these headings.

### Added
- Upkeep: each dawn you are in the world for, your settlement pays for itself and the screen says so ("Upkeep paid: 46 Regals"). It is a sum for the town's size (nothing for a Hamlet, 8 for a Town, 24 for a City, 48 for a Castle), 2 Regals for every building and 3 for every guard. A new settlement pays nothing for its first three days, and days you are away are never charged. If you can't pay, what you have is taken, nothing is knocked down, and fallen guards are not retrained until a dawn is paid in full. If another player wears the Crown, a fifth of what you pay goes to them. The Almanac has a page on it.
- A Breeder's Post that stands in a town now sells that region's breed straight away, with no setting up.
- Works plots: the Ironworks is no longer placed by hand. A town laid out with a Works plot keeps that ground for it: use the plot's marker, pick the Ironworks, and it builds itself there in your region's look, fenced at its full size from the first day with trees on the ground it hasn't built on yet. The Bloomery and the Trip-Hammer Forge then rise on the same plot from the Ironworks block in the yard. You choose the plot, never the exact spot. Older towns with no Works plot still place the crafted block as before.
- The Hollow's air is full of floating white specks now: hundreds of tiny pale flecks hang all round you and drift slowly every which way, wherever you go down there.
- A quest tracker in the top-right corner, under the minimap, is always on screen now. It shows the story step you are on (find Sister Wren, your settlement's mystery, the next boss's puzzle, Fennick's notes, the Tuning Fork, the Rift, and the way on from there), a hint, and how far away it is and which way to turn. A second line shows a side step when there is one, such as founding your settlement. Each player sees their own; type /siege:quests to hide or show it. Hidden places stay hidden: it points to the nearest town and gives a hint instead. The Almanac has a page on it.
- The Everwake Wings: use them once and they are bound to you for good, worn over any armour (full Choirsteel included), and they never break. Jump, then jump again in the air, to open them and glide like an elytra; press Use with an item in hand while gliding, not looking at a block, for a silent burst of speed. They won't open in the Hollow and say why. For now only `/scriptevent siege:wings_give` (creative) gives them; the Everwake storyline that earns them comes later.
- Under the Seat there is no longer just empty void. Far below lies a glowing chasm: a pool of light, falls pouring out of the cliffs, and crooked crystal spires rising from the water, the tallest right under the Seat. Cavern walls rise round it to the roof, with falls of light behind the throne, so the Seat floats inside one great closed cavern. Falling in is still the end of you.
- Real recordings replace the stand-in sounds for 29 signature moments: the 3:17 toll, the siege horn, the choir hum, the Hollow's ambience, the storm tide, every boss's arrival, the role storyline sounds, three dragon roars, and the Rift's opening (the Tuning Fork's answer, the rock stirring, the hush, the breath, the open Rift's draught, the veil pushing you back, crossing over, the seep creeping, and the draught at a thin place).
- Every town station the map places has a road to the town: Sunscar's new main street runs out through its wall gate to the station, and Millbrook, Mirelight and Rimehold get roads from their stations to their nearest streets.
- A plot that is the last one your next charter can use is now kept for it: the Plot Marker greys the other buildings with "Kept for the Mint (Town charter)" (and the like), and a hand-built building can't be counted there instead, so a settlement can never block its own growth. Rival lords follow the same rule. Houses can also go on long Medium plots.
- The Farmhouse, Inn and Mint can now be raised on plots from their Plot Markers (the farmhouse also fits a long Medium plot), and houses and townhouses count as homes for the charters. Every town layout was redone: each keeps a 9 by 9 Mason's Yard by the square, and the plots the level 4 town hall will need are marked like the level 5 ones (the hall pays back a building there in full and your next plot of that size is free). Rival lords keep their bigger plots free for what their next charter needs.
- After the Choir, the game now tells you where to go. The chat says the way on is south, up the Long Climb, to the Seat. The Court scene ends by pointing you through the open gate behind the throne and to the Carter. Until Morvane falls, the action bar in the Hollow shows how far away the Seat is and which way to turn.
- The Choir fight is harder and busier. The Echoes of the Siege now come in waves for the whole fight: kill every one and a bigger wave rises a few seconds later, with something worse walking among them every few waves (vexes, a ravager, a witch). The Conductor no longer stands still: it glides around the hall and slips away when you get close. The screen now says plainly that the seventh Singer can't be hurt until the Conductor is low, when it comes down to the middle.
- The Hollow can be seen far off now. It used to be built only about 48 blocks round you, so past that you saw a flat purple wall; now the land is built out to about 200 blocks, the way you are looking first, then the rest of the Hollow fills in while anyone is down there, whatever your simulation distance. Its roof is raised to nether height, about 100 blocks over the land, with longer hanging rock and Veilmoss curtains (places you have already been get the new roof as you come near). Purple clouds drift in the top of the cavern, the fog is a little thicker, and the storm's lightning strikes further off, where you can see it.
- Castle gates and portcullises have their own sounds: the gate groans open on its hinges and booms shut, the portcullis rattles up on its chain and slams down. (Stand-ins for now; real recordings follow.)
- A new settlement now comes with its Mason's Yard: a stone workshop in the region's style with the Mason's Bench in its front yard, where the Mason works (he no longer stands on a bare bench). You also get a cottage of your own with a bed kept for you, so you can sleep the first night. The settlement's road now runs out through the gate and joins the nearest map road at its own height. The ground round a new settlement is filled and sloped, so no path or pad floats or stands on a dirt step, and plot buildings sit on their plot's stone instead of a raised pad. Doors, lanterns, signs, ladders and other attached blocks are now placed after a building's walls, so they no longer break as it rises.
- The bell that rings when something is finished (your hall, your wall, a boss beaten, a territory won) now tolls three times instead of once.
- Every region has its own weather now. Mist rises off the Blackfen at dusk and in the rain, and some nights small lights drift out over the fen. Saltreach gets a hard wind off the sea that carries spray, and a white sea fret at first light. The Emberwaste has dust winds and sandstorms you can barely see through. Snow blows through the Frostmarch, and blizzards close the passes. The Vale keeps its rain and gets mist in the low ground some mornings. Weather builds up slowly and thins out after a few minutes. It is only weather and never hurts you. Under a roof it thins to a grey light, and down a cave it can't reach you. The action bar tells you when something is coming in, and the Almanac has a Weather page.
- The Faceless Gate's processional way now has real statues: eight robed, hooded, faceless figures about 20 blocks tall on stepped plinths. Four stand south of the scarab court, holding a staff, a bowl or folded hands, and one has lost its hood. The other four lie toppled and broken along the precinct walls.
- The ground across the whole map is now mixed block by block instead of lying in flat patches: the Blackfen's grass is shot through with moss, podzol, mud and roots under moss carpet and ferns, the Vale's grass with coarse dirt, podzol and moss, Saltreach with gravel and stone, the tundra with podzol and stone, and the desert with the odd sandstone. Snowy peaks no longer have blue packed-ice dots. Plots and towns fade back to plain ground at their edges.
- Every boss site is now a whole place on the map, not just a monument. The Cradle Tree is twice the size, on a raised knoll, with roots sprawling across the fen, black-water pools and the sunken Changeling Glade on the way to its cave. Brannoc's bell tower rises from the sea with a slate spire over drowned cottages, cloister arches, a churchyard islet and a broken causeway to its door. Hallowmere's ice is now mostly snow, so you no longer slide about, with drifts, pressure ridges and snowy hills round it. The Faceless Gate has moved out of Sunscar's houses into the desert south of town, a sunken walled courtyard among great dunes. Mirelight's square has a lantern-house with a great caged light, and Millbrook a proper clock tower over the east street with the Tower clock on its face.
- The regional boss sites can be seen from far off: a crown of great bone-and-ice antlers rising out of Hallowmere round the Pale Huntsman's ice, the Drowned Bride's bell tower standing up out of Gull Bay with the churchyard's broken pillars lit beneath it, a faceless colossus seated behind twin pylons over the Ash Seneschal's vault stair, the vast Cradle Tree over the Mother of Changelings' cave with empty cradles hanging from its boughs, and a tall lantern mast in Mirelight's square.
- The three sealed ways out now have something to find: the old kingdom's gate across the Shut Pass, shut behind its portcullis with the rockfall and snow that buried the way piled through the bars; the Westwatch on the west coast, a barred sea arch and a lighthouse that has never been lit, looking toward the Far Shore; and the Southmost Light on the south shore, a tower with a great copper spyglass trained on the Horizon Isles and a ring of stones beside it. Each builds itself when someone first comes near.
- Vorlund's Muster: the Grey Host's war camp on the north-east Frostmarch plateau, a palisade ring round a fighting ground with gate towers and signal fires, and an upturned longship for a hall with its mast and ragged grey sail raised through the keel, seen from far off.
- Every creature has a voice of its own now. Mobs, bosses and the rival lords' champions used to grunt like a player when hit; now the Grey Host mutter low, rimewolves growl, mire toads croak deep, the Clockwork Reeve ticks, the Drowned Bride hums, and most creatures make a quiet idle sound now and then (rarer than vanilla, so a sound in the dark means something). Your soldiers and townsfolk sound human, siege engines creak and splinter, and the Standing Ranks stay silent. Crystal chimes, the bell's hum and a few other sounds that played nothing at all in the Hollow, the vault and the reach now play.
- Every regional boss now has a fight on the way in, like the Changeling Glade: rimewolves come across the ice of Hallowmere after dark, the drowned of Brannoc swim up at anyone in the water round the sunken chapel, ash scarabs boil out of the sand in front of the Faceless Vault's stair, and once the Millbrook well is open, footmen climb the stair to meet whoever comes down. More come for a bigger party, and each place rests five minutes after a wave.
- The seven thin places are seven different caves now. King's Barrow is a long stone-lined passage into a beehive chamber; the Bellfounders' Mine a timbered adit and a steep stair down to a pillared hall; Frostvein a winding crevice into a tall ice shaft; the Drowned Chapel cut stairs and a swim into a hall; Witchroot a low winding root tunnel; Saltglass a wide gallery into a tall crystal hall; the Sunken Quarry a short cut and a swim into a tall squared gallery. In every one the Rift's wall is the same, and the land now rises over the cave as a hill instead of a block of rock. Fennick's camp always finds level ground.
- The Rift opens properly: forty seconds from the strike. A low bell answers from inside the stone, the chamber trembles harder and harder, everything goes quiet, then the split climbs the wall from floor to ceiling and pulls apart, and air pours out. The crack now runs the whole height of the wall and opens on a void: veil after veil going back into the dark.
- The veil only takes those who earned it. You can step through only once all five regional bosses have fallen by your own hand; anyone else is pushed gently back and told how many they still owe. Coming back out is always free.
- Once the Rift is open, the Hollow seeps into our world as veins: connected, branching scars of Veilmoss that creep out from the cave mouth, reaching about 16 blocks after two weeks, 48 after four, and then two more blocks every day for as long as the Rift stays open. A building in the wild that a vein reaches turns to the Hollow's own stone and ash. Town walls and claimed plots hold it back.
- The real thin place now gives itself away with small amethyst buds coming up round the seam over the weeks (no more sculk), and its draught pulls toward the wall.
- The Plan Book's cards are redrawn from the buildings as they look now, including the new House, Townhouse, Farmhouse, Mint and Inn, and the screen has room for all of them.
- After Morvane falls, the Deep Door in the Vault finally opens. Far below, the Grey Host the Hollow swallowed three hundred years ago is still standing in its ranks, held there by Ulfa, the Bell-Wife, who has not stopped ringing. Beat her in the Bell Room (she drops Ulfa's Bell and comes back stronger each day) and the Ranks open: talk to each company's serjeant to lead them out to stand at your keep (2 more guards each), lay them to rest for Veil Ash and a keepsake, or leave them standing.
- Line trains: every railway line with track laid now has its own train (an engine and two coaches) that runs by itself from one end of the laid track to the other and back, standing 20 seconds at each stop and calling out the stop and the next one. Anyone can ride it, free. It can't be put away or given more coaches. With nobody near, it keeps moving along the line unseen and appears when you get close. `/scriptevent siege:trains lines` says where each one is.
- Passing loops: from the Station stage up, every settlement station has a second track beside its platform, joined to the line by a switch at each end. A train that finds another standing at the platform takes the loop and passes it. A Halt stays single track. Stations already built get their loop the next time they grow.
- The way to the Seat is built out. The Long Climb ends in a ragged, crumbling cliff with the path running out on its own spur to the Last Step, a broken gate. From there you walk (no jumping) across five floating rocks joined by hollowwood roots, curving down to the Seat, with broken rock, giant Veilcaps and soul lanterns adrift in the fog round you. The six stepping stones are gone.
- Playing alone, the mentors' students now turn up and fight beside you in the last fight: Hrafn at the Standing Ranks, Dame Orla and a knight of the Eleven on the Night It Ended, Jory Holt with a crossbow at the River, and Senna Vey on her own dragon, circling the Rift and burning Morvane. And when you are the only Dragon Rider about and take to the air on a siege night, Kesh's cadets fly at your wing on their own dragons and burn raiders near you: Senna from the start, Pim Ashgrove too once you are Wing-Sworn.
- Trains keep apart: a train that comes up behind another on the same track slows and stops a few blocks short of it instead of running through it, and the driver is told a train is on the line ahead.
- The Changeling Glade: on the way down to the Mother of Changelings' cave, the sunken glade south of the Cradle Tree sends a wave of changelings at whoever walks through it (more for a bigger party), then rests for five minutes.
- Siege crews go to war with your army, each bringing its own engine: Trebuchet crews set up 40 blocks out, unfold their trebuchet and widen one hole in the wall nearest the vault (a boulder every 45 seconds, never at the gate); Ram crews ram the gate; Sappers plant Breaching Charges and run. Train them at the Barracks.
- Soldiers fight by role in a siege: Shieldbearers cover the crews, Crossbowmen and Archers hang back, Knights ride down archers, everyone else pours through the breach; defending Pikemen and Shieldbearers brace the gate and breach.
- New soldiers walk out of the Barracks to the yard; new Archers and Crossbowmen go straight to an empty post on the wall.
- The Grand Station's glass train shed now has stone arches between its pillars on both sides.
- Every building is furnished inside now, not just the inn. Plot homes get a living room with a hearth, a table and chairs, and bedrooms upstairs or under the roof with a chest by every bed; townhouses and farmhouses the same, the farmhouse with a kitchen; the inn a bar, tables and guest beds; the mint a counting counter and chests. Every floor has a proper staircase (or a ladder in narrow towers) with a rail round the stairwell, lanterns so nothing spawns indoors, and there is always a clear one-block path from the door to every room, bed and chest. Each region furnishes in its own way: red rugs and spruce in the Vale, furs in Frostmarch, birch and sea charts in Saltreach, jars and mangrove in Blackfen, low tables and bright cushions in the Emberwaste.
- Gull's Rest looks like a town that remembers a sunken city: houses of pale stone and quartz columns on sea-worn prismarine, with sea-glass windows, and roofs that never repeat down a street: low hips, mansards, bell roofs, domed houses with a quartz deck, temple fronts with a colonnade, houses with a roof terrace, and houses with a slim corner tower. Roofs come in sea green, teal, white and copper.
- War Writs: click another player's Keep Banner to declare war (320 Regals) if they hold a relic, attacked your settlement, refused your demand or are an Oathbreaker, or send them a demand for a tenth of their Regals. With no grounds it is an unjust war: triple the fee, their defences twice as strong, and you are an Oathbreaker for three days whom anyone may declare on. Their gates shut at once; the assault comes at the next nightfall and lasts until dawn, online or not. Walls, vault and Regal Safe can be broken, their guards hunt the attackers, and the Keep Banner can't be taken. Crack the vault before dawn to win; otherwise the defenders win. Three days of peace follow.
- Your army goes to war with you: on declaring, a slider picks how much of it rides with you (0 to 100%). They mount horses and follow your trail, go round rivers by a bridge rather than swimming, dismount near the enemy and gather at their gate, then attack at nightfall while the other side's guards fight back.
- Train soldiers straight from the Barracks menu (pick how many of each type, see the total) and upgrade the Barracks to make them cheaper. Fallen soldiers retrain by themselves for half their price, paid to whoever killed them.
- Off-duty soldiers stroll round the Barracks yard (and now and then the town) by day and sleep inside the Barracks at night; a raid, a siege or an assault wakes them at once.
- The Barracks Armoury upgrades the armour of a whole type of soldier at once; soldiers trained later come out wearing it.
- New soldiers to train at the Barracks as your settlement grows: Pikemen, Shieldbearers, Crossbowmen and mounted Knights, each dressed for your region and in your banner colour.
- Seven new soldier looks for the Barracks and Siege Workshop: Pikeman (pike), Shieldbearer (tower shield), Crossbowman (crossbow, pavise on his back), Knight (lance, kite shield and crest, rides a horse), Ram Crew, Trebuchet Crew and Sapper (charge satchel), each with its own moves (bracing the pike, raising the shield, aiming, couching the lance, swinging the ram, loading the trebuchet, planting a charge). Every soldier, the old Man-at-Arms and Archer included, now dresses for his settlement's region (Vale, Frostmarch furs, Saltreach sailcloth, Emberwaste head wraps, Blackfen mud and hoods, the Hollow's violet) and wears his banner colour where you can always see it: the tabard, an armband over any armour, and his shield, pennon or pack. Armour looks different on every type and in every region too: a Pikeman's kettle hat and banded plate, a Crossbowman's wide brim and leather sleeves, a Shieldbearer's big pauldrons, a Knight's trimmed plate and rondels, crews in leather pads and aprons, and finishes from Frostmarch blued steel to blackened Blackfen iron, at all five tiers. Guards salute with a hand to the brow.
- The Barracks is rebuilt as a small castle in every region's style: a timber hall with a mess below and bunks above, a tall round keep with battlements and flags joined to it, an outside stair up to the keep, and a walled yard with corner turrets where your soldiers wait when they aren't on duty. A banner in your settlement's colour hangs over the gate, and the keep flies flags in it. The yard fits the ground it lands on: where one of your paths already crosses its wall, the wall leaves a gap and the path runs on into the yard.
- No rail line in the Hollow: the minecart rail beside the road from Cartmeet to the Seams is gone (the Hollow has no trains). Already-explored stretches keep what was built.
- Two new homes for town plots: the House (a Medium plot, one storey and a tall attic, about four beds) and the taller Townhouse (a long Medium or Large plot, stone below and a jettied floor over the road, about six beds). Each region builds them its own way: timber frames in the Vale, dark oak and slate with dragon-head gables in Frostmarch, sandstone and whitewash under sea-green hipped roofs in Saltreach, flat-roofed sandstone with roof beams in Emberwaste, and mud brick on mangrove piles in Blackfen.
- The regional boss sites can be seen from far off: a crown of great bone-and-ice antlers rising out of Hallowmere round the Pale Huntsman's ice, the Drowned Bride's bell tower standing up out of Gull Bay with the churchyard's broken pillars lit beneath it, a faceless colossus seated behind twin pylons over the Ash Seneschal's vault stair, the vast Cradle Tree over the Mother of Changelings' cave with empty cradles hanging from its boughs, and a tall lantern mast in Mirelight's square.
- Every plot's old landmark now stands on the map, just as its local papers describe it: the Grey Host's rusting iron in the Harrowford meadow and the ferryman's post on the Alder, the leaning oaks over the Oakmere cellar, the empty bell frame on Thistledown with its sheep pens, the burned house and the apple tree with the E by Wren Water, the copper road at Frosthelm, the Wolfcairn cairn, the Gullcliff beacon and the watch room in the chalk under it, the measuring posts walking into the sea at Tidewatch, the ring of nine posts at Reedmoor, the lantern posts on Lanternbog, the forty pale stones on Redmesa and the forty chained desks and nineteen palms at Dunehold. Each builds itself the first time someone comes near, on any map world, so nothing needs regenerating.
- The story's places now stand where the Ledger says they are: the roofless Hall of Bells and broken walls of Crownhold with the King's crack in the cellar, the Seal at Bellhollow with half the split Founding Bell beside it and the pilgrims' bells by the shrine, the Ashen Gate chapterhouse with Brother Cael among the books and the Burned Archive under the floor, the Ashen Watch over the fen, Corvin's Tower with its empty bell frame, Old Mag's hut on its stilts, Nell Sedge's raft-house in the reeds, the Deepforge cut into the mountain with Ulla at Hroda's fire, and the Cinder Roost on its cliff with the Span, the perch tower, Kesh, and the names of the fallen. Like the plot landmarks, each builds itself when someone first comes near, and the people who live there are standing in them.
- The Hollow's last two fight spaces are rebuilt: the Drowned Court is now King Oswin's flooded round hall, with coursed veinstone and dark prismarine, pilasters and soul-lit niches, drowned banners, eight sea-lit columns, knee-deep channels across a patterned floor, and the throne on its dais before the sealed gate. The Seat is now a veinstone ring in the fog with seven Singer pillars crowned in crystal, choir ore in the disc, the throne Morvane waits before, and the disc's root hanging into the dark below. Worlds that built the old look rebuild them the next time someone comes near.
- **Letters by messenger**: type `/siege:messenger` (or use the Keep menu) to send another player a private letter. A messenger takes it there in a minute and a half to three minutes, depending on how far apart you are. While it rides, a small countdown above your hotbar says "Your message is being delivered". If they are away, it waits for them to come back.
- **The Crier**: big moments across the realm now show up in chat, marked [Crier]. These include a boss waking (and who is fighting it), a boss beating everyone who came for it, new settlements, newcomers, plots claimed, walls raised, stations opening, lords being called out, feuds ending, dragons bonding or dying, a settlement's first train, siege-night streaks (3, 5, 10 and every 5 after, and a long streak lost), special offers at Trade Outposts, mysteries solved, and players falling to bosses, creatures or each other out in the wild (never on someone's land). Attacks on players' settlements are never announced, because only the Messenger brings those.
- **Messenger**, a new skill in the Keep branch of the Tree of Oaths (first ring). Without it, you hear nothing about attacks on your settlement while you are away: everything that happened is read out when you next step onto your own land. With it, a messenger finds you the moment anyone attacks your settlement (players hitting your walls, guards or people, raiders marching on your keep, a rival lord's raid, a vault break-in), tells you what is happening and which way home lies. Swear it for 1 skill point or pay 100 Regals; Regals paid are kept through unbinding your oaths. New Almanac page "News from home: the Messenger".
- The Trade Outpost has its own building in every region's style, growing with your station: a timber goods hall in the Vale, a steep-roofed dark-log store in the Frostmarch, a whitewashed hall under a sea-green hip roof in Saltreach, a flat-roofed sandstone kasbah store in the Emberwaste and a mangrove and mud-brick hall in Blackfen, each with a striped awning over the loading hatch and barrels by the wall.
- Special Offers at the Trade Board: choose Create Special Offer, pick a good and how many, see what it's worth at the normal rate and name a better price. Your Regals are held until it's filled and the Crier announces it. Other settlements can fill it by train, towns and lords fill good offers, and anyone can walk up to your Trade Board with the goods and hand them over in person to be paid on the spot. Unfilled offers refund after 3 days.
- Gravestones: when you die, everything you drop goes into a gravestone where you fell instead of lying on the ground. Click the headstone to gather it all back (armour goes straight back on). For 10 minutes only you can open it, while its candle burns; then the candle goes out and anyone can. If another player killed you, your grave is open to anyone at once and your killer is told where it is. Graves can't be broken or blown up, and an emptied grave settles back into the earth. There is a page in the Almanac.
- The Plan Book: a Plot Marker only offers buildings you know the plan for. The cottage, well and library are known from the start, your town hall teaches more as it grows, and the rest come as Plan Scrolls from toppled Rival Lords, bosses and Rumor jobs. Plans from other regions come in their own style and cost half as much again to raise. Copy a plan onto a scroll at your Plan Book to trade it. The Plan Book is a screen of building cards (also a button in the Town Hall menu): found plans bright, missing ones greyed with where to find them, and a click shows price, worker, charter and other regions' styles.
- A bought stretch of line never tears down a building: its bed only digs through ground and trees, and if a building stands in the way the line goes down around it and the buyer is told where.
- Trade Outposts: once your station is on the line and you own an engine, build a Trade Outpost from the station menu. Set up standing trades with other settlements, Rival Lords and the towns at its Trade Board ("20 iron for 4 gold, every day"); both sides must accept, either can counter, and new requests float above the board in the sender's colour. Your train runs each route while you're away, taking from your Exports barrel and filling your Imports barrel, and pauses when either side runs short. Kingsrest buys any trade good for Regals at a set price, and Goods Wagons carry more each run. Watch out for bandits on long lines.
- The Vault: choose "Vault" in the Town Hall menu, pay, and you get the Vault Key. Use it on the ground on your land where the stairs should start: a staircase is carved down to an entrance hall and a Wooden Strongroom underground, with a 3x3 vault door and the Vault Lock beside it. Click the lock with the key and the door swings open like a gate (click again to shut it). Keys are cut for one vault: you and your allies open yours with any key, anyone else needs a key cut for it. Hold a relic and click a plinth inside to set it floating there; click it to take it back. Click the lock with an iron ingot, then a diamond, a netherite ingot and a Choirsteel ingot to reinforce it: each tier rebuilds the room bigger with a tougher door. Vault Breakers set against the lock now drill through it (only while the owner is online); when the lock gives, the door falls. Cracked locks and doors mend with the vault's material, and a fallen door is rebuilt from the Vault menu. A keeper away a full real day with a relic leaves their vault open to break-ins for two random half-hours a day (announced in game and on Discord), while their walls, vault and Keep stand twice as strong and their guards fight harder; any login starts the day again. Every vault has a Regal Safe (2 wide, 3 high, can't be mined or moved) holding all your Regals; a thief who gets in breaks it open a blow at a time, each blow paying out a share (shown as a number) and the last one the rest. Better vaults take far more blows and pay less each, and a broken safe is announced to the whole server.
- Regals are now one sum per player: no coins in your pockets, no strongbox, nothing to deposit or withdraw. Coins you pick up are counted in at once, shops take from the sum and rewards add to it anywhere. The Keep menu's Regals button shows it. The Moneyer no longer makes change.
- Townsfolk now walk the streets of Kingsrest (20 of them) and of Millbrook, Rimehold, Gull's Rest, Sunscar and Mirelight (10 each). Click one and they stop, turn to you and talk: everyone has five things to say, heard in order, from gossip and jokes to things worth remembering. They go indoors at night and are back at first light.
- Rail gates: where a line passes through a wall, the gate bars or wall across the track open for an oncoming train and shut behind it. Players on foot can't get through.
- The miniature map in the welcome hall now sits on a white cloud with a puffy, cloud-shaped edge instead of a square stone rim, and its Emberwaste has low dunes, banded red mesas, the odd acacia and dead bushes instead of a flat sheet of sand.
