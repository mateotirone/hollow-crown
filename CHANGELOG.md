# Changelog

What changed in each version of The Hollow Crown, newest first. Written for players: say what they will notice, not which files moved.

How versions work is in [`docs/RELEASING.md`](docs/RELEASING.md).

## Unreleased

Everything merged to `main` since the last release goes here. Each pull request that changes what players see adds a line under one of these headings.

### Added
- The Quartermaster's Armoury is no longer crafted: raise it from the Plot Marker on a Medium plot (every Hamlet has one besides the Barracks plot), for 24 Regals, and a quartermaster takes the post at its desk. Buildings are never placed with a block now: the Hall Cornerstone comes with your Hamlet, your station rises by itself at the Station Post near your plot, and the Foundation Stone, Hall Cornerstone, Rail Depot and Quartermaster's Desk recipes are gone (the Mason no longer sells Foundation Stones, and the town hall can't be moved). Blocks already placed or in your pack keep working.
- **Watchtowers on your wall.** From the first wall (the Low Palisade) up to the Battlement Wall, four small watchtowers stand astride your wall line in your region's style: a stone base, a ladder inside up to a hatch, and an open lookout under the region's tower roof. Each lookout is a guard post ("Watchtower, north-east"), and new Archers climb the towers before they walk the wall. At the Watch Wall the stone towers rise in the same four places. Walls you already have get their towers the next time the world loads.
- The Barracks is no longer crafted: it opens with your Hamlet and you raise it from the Plot Marker on your big empty plot (every Hamlet has one that fits), for 16 Regals. The working Barracks block stands by its door when it's built. Barracks blocks already placed keep working.
- The first-nights tracker spells out every step on screen, with a pin to the plot for your Barracks: build it from its Plot Marker, wait for it to go up, click its Barracks block to train guards, then hold the first siege.
- A way home from the Hollow: walk into the tear at the back of the Wound and you come out at the Rift, or back where you came in if the Rift isn't open. Testers can type `/scriptevent hc:undertow_leave`.
- **"You are entering ..."** A title card now shows the place's name and a short line when you walk into one of the six towns (Kingsrest, Millbrook, Rimehold, Gull's Rest, Sunscar, Mirelight), one of the Order's houses, Bellhollow, Crownhold, a story landmark such as the Cinder Roost or the Deepforge, a Wonder, or another player's settlement ("Ada's Village"). Walking into your own settlement says "You are home". It shows once on the way in, never while you walk along an edge, and the same place waits a minute and a half before it shows again. It replaces the old action-bar line for settlements; free plots and the Citadel keep theirs.
- A new quest step, "Find your railway station", points you to your settlement's Halt (and shows on the tracker with the distance and direction) until you have stood on its platform. It shows even during the first nights.
- No NPC reads just "NPC" any more: each one gets its own name back (or a plain one like Settler or Miner), and the "Click to talk" label now sits above an NPC's name instead of on top of it.
- Every NPC now has a face of their own. The Mason, Sister Wren and the other story people, settlers, fair traders and travelling players, the wonder keepers, the clock keepers, Old Sigga, the Mirelight villagers, rival lords (dressed for their region and oath), the guest-night visitors, the Hollow's foremen and miners and the great houses' envoys no longer wear Bedrock's stock NPC skins. NPCs already standing in your world change over the next time you are near them.
- **Storehouse.** Every settlement now gets its own Storehouse the moment the Hamlet is laid out, ready to use straight away: a Store Shed with 27 chests set sideways in bays between stone pillars. It is rebuilt bigger on the same ground each time your town grows a tier (54 chests at Town, 108 over two floors at City, 144 over three at Castle), in your region's style, and everything in it is carried out and put back in the same chests while it is rebuilt. A Storekeeper moves in to work it. The old Storehouse plot building (Castle only) is no longer offered; one you already built stays.
- **Goods Crates.** A barrel stands beside the post of every station your settlement owns. Put things in it and they go home on the next goods train: they turn up in your Storehouse's chests the next time you are there. If the Storehouse is full, what doesn't fit waits at its door until there is room.
- The starter village now keeps a 15 by 15 piece of ground free beside the Mason's Yard for the Storehouse, so it never lands on the Barracks plot or a later building.
- Every player settlement now has its own Farmer, standing outside the second house of your Hamlet. He sells seeds and buys your wheat, so you can earn your first Regals at home. Settlements founded before this get their Farmer the next time you go home.
- A new step on the quest tracker, "Sell 16 wheat to the Farmer", points you to your Farmer with a pin on the minimap and a gold mark over his head until you've sold him 16 wheat.
- Starter clothes never wear out now.
- **Starter horse.** Every new player is given a horse of their own once, saddled and ready, with a Horse Whistle: a working cob from the region they start in (Vale Plough-Cob, Frostmarch Sledge-Cob, Saltreach Cart-Cob, Emberwaste Pack-Cob or Blackfen Peat-Cob), each in its own coat and saddle blanket. It is slower than any horse you can earn and can't jump a fence, can't be trained or shod, and doesn't take one of your stalls. Blow the whistle and it comes from anywhere; if it falls, it limps home after a day. The quest tracker points it out until you first ride it. Players who already started get theirs the next time they join.
- Every player starts with 50 Regals. Players who already started get them the next time they join.
- Crops, saplings and sugar cane grow about three times faster: the world's random tick speed is now 3 instead of 1. Grass spreads, leaves decay and fire spreads faster too.
- The recipe book's Equipment tab now lists only the regional armour and guard gear of the region you're standing in, instead of all five regions' near-identical pieces. Walk into another region and its pieces appear instead. Any region's piece can still be crafted by placing the items yourself.
- Your Regal balance is always on screen now: a small gold Regal and the number, just right of the hotbar. It changes the moment you are paid, spend, pick up coins or get robbed.
- Two new player skins come with the add-on in their own skin pack, The Hollow Crown: Reikia Cinderhale of the Emberwaste in caravaneer's clothes, and Lorcan Duskmire of the Blackfen in a fen-walker's oilskins. Pick them in the dressing room once the pack is imported.
- The Faceless Vault's clues now match the vault: the Ledger, the warden, townsfolk and the notebooks put it in the dunes south of Sunscar and tell you to set its five statues in the order of the king's life, instead of rebuilding a face under the town.
- The story's places now match the ground they stand on. Millbrook stays in the Reeve's long night even by day, a dark blue fog inside the town that lifts for good once he falls, and its chapel and clock tower have empty bell frames with bare chains where the bells were taken down. A ruined watchtower stands over the north pass where the Vale road climbs into the Frostmarch, half of it fallen, with Chronicle II lying on the chest inside. Hallowmere has its shrine: a standing stone on the south-east shore with a full moon carved on its lake face, a fishing hut out on the ice, and further west a white moon lying under the ice round a hatch that opens on the night our moon is dark, down a shaft to a refuge in the lake bed.
- When the map is next generated, the plots' old landmarks get the ground their story describes: a river bend past the Harrowford ferry, black water ringing Lanternbog's island, an oasis pool at Dunehold, a red mesa under the Redmesa stones, a grassy knoll for Thistledown's bell frame, a broken plateau edge behind Wolfcairn, a crag behind Frosthelm's road, a sandy inlet to the Tidewatch posts and a chalk zawn under the Gullcliff beacon.
- The Rider's trial on the Cinder Roost now names the Span over the gorge correctly.
- Only the Drowned Bride's sunken chapel off Gull's Rest is Saint Brannoc's now. The thin place on the north cliffs is just the Drowned Chapel.
- The minimap shows a gold pin for your current quest, the same place the quest tracker points at. When it is off the edge of the map, the pin waits on the rim on the side it lies. When the quest sends you to talk to someone (Sister Wren, the clock keepers, Old Sigga, the Carter), a gold "!" floats over their head once you are near, only for you, and the minimap pin moves onto them. Hiding the quest tracker (/siege:quests) hides these too.
- Arriving on one of the twelve map plots now gives you a real starter village: a paved square, a main lane out through the gate and back lanes either side of the hall, the library on the square, two houses already standing (one of them yours, with your bed and the founder's chest), the Mason's Yard, a woodyard, a pen, a common field, the hall green and an open plot big enough for the Barracks, all on graded ground. The wall follows the land round it instead of a circle, and every ring after it keeps the same shape. The town grows round the village from Town on, and the Town wall now stands further out (about 54 blocks instead of 46) so the Town's plots fit. (A settlement founded before this update gets the new layout's plots but not the village's ground: start a fresh world to see it.)
- Choir Shards are now five different shards, one from each regional boss, and the Tuning Fork needs one of each (two of the same won't do). Everyone who fought the boss gets their own shard straight into their pack, with a reminder to keep it. The quest tracker shows which shards you still need, and old one-size shards in your pack turn into the ones you're missing.
- The Codex: a book of every rare thing in the realm. Anything you hold is drawn in full with what it does; anything you haven't held shows only its outline and a hint. Every ten finds pays 50 Regals, a full chapter earns a title. Open it from the book (new players get one; craft another from a book and an item frame), the Keep menu or /siege:codex. With it come 75 new things to find: armour and two weapons for each of the five regions (forged at the wonders from a regional alloy, and only wearable by people settled in that region or holding its Kin-Writ), the Oathsworn harness from the five regional bosses, relics from claimants, rival champions, the regent and the Warlord King, twelve mythical weapons and tools hidden around the map, seven charms at the thin places, and the Sun Gate caravan market (four goods at a time, new stalls every three days) with its Caravan Banner.
- The ruined air boat station stands on the north shore of Wren Water, south of Kingsrest: a broken hall and mooring mast on a yard cut into the shore, with a lamp-lit lane out to the road. One day it will fly again.
- The minimap works in the Hollow: its own map of the land down there, with the light rivers, roads and named places, and labels for where you are (the stretch of the story path, a village or site, or the far country). It goes blank during a Slip.
- The Hollow has a stronghold: the Needlehold, a crooked round fortress in the wood country west of the Long Climb, beside a light river. It has a moat of glowing water, two rings of walls, two dozen tall leaning needle towers that don't match, a cluster of spires round a throne hall, and Veilcap beds and giant Veilcaps outside. Every tower floor is a room you can walk into (guard rooms with bunks, stores, armouries, watch rooms), all reached by spiral stairs. A road leaves the Long Climb and runs down to its gate. It builds itself in pieces as you come near.
- The Hollow sounds more alive: now and then a single far-off sound reaches you from somewhere you can't see, like singing a long way off, water dripping from the roof, wind breathing through a crack, or rock settling. Light rivers hum as you come near, a bell rings somewhere under the Drowned Court's water, and the Seat is almost silent. The same sound never plays twice in a row.
- The Hollow's air changes from place to place: a thicker violet haze hangs round the Wound where you arrive, a cold blue-grey haze lies over the Drowned Court and its sunken towers, the light rivers glow brighter as you come near, and the Seat is still and quiet, with dark clear air, specks that barely move, no clouds and no thunder. Each change fades in over a long way, never a wall.
- The Twelve Gates, a grand round arena, now stands just south-west of Kingsrest's grand station, down a lamp-lit paved way from the station door. Its round sand floor is ringed by twelve starting rooms spaced evenly round it, one in each settlement colour, each with a portcullis that lifts onto the floor, the default kit on racks and a board to pick your kit and start. Twenty-five rows of round stands lit for night fights, a royal box, four round domed stair towers up to the top promenade, a gallery with views over Kingsrest, and an entrance hall with the two challenge boards. (Needs the next map rebuild.)
- The Twelve Gates, the arena at Kingsrest. Click a board in its entrance hall to battle another King (one on one, everyone for themselves, or up to four teams, up to 12 fighters in the twelve settlement-coloured rooms) or a beast. Invited Kings get a message wherever they are and saying yes takes them to their room. In your room, fight with your own things or take the arena kit (the same iron kit for everyone; your own things wait in a locker), press Start Battle, and when everyone is ready the portcullises rise, the same castle portcullis as on settlement walls (nobody can click them open early). A crowd fills the stands, cheering and groaning, but only while a fight is on. Beasts pay Regals by beast and difficulty (Squire to Legend). Nothing is lost: whatever you drop when you fall is picked up for you, and afterwards everyone is healed, given their things and game mode back, and sent back where they came from. Creative players fight in Adventure mode.
- **Dragon colours.** Every dragon now hatches in one of three colours for its kind: Cinderwings come in Ember Red, Ash Black or Copper, Ashen Wyverns in Ashen Grey, Bone White or Soot, Dune Drakes in Dune Gold, Rust or Pale Sand, and Glass Wyrms in Amethyst Glass, Sea Glass or Frost Glass. The colour shows in its name on the sheet. Wild dragons at the landmarks come in all three colours too. Dragons you already have stay their first colour.
- **Dragon Stable.** Place a Dragon Roost on your land and a kasbah stable for dragons rises behind it: a wide arched way in, stalls off a broad aisle, a tack corner, a hay loft and a flat roof to land on. Joined on the east, under a small blue-green dome, is the nursery with three **Hatching Braziers**. Dragon eggs now hatch there: set one in a brazier and you can see it sitting in the glowing coals, and it starts to crack before it hatches. The Roost is still where you train your dragon. The Roost can now be crafted from things you can actually find (acacia, a gold block, hay, a campfire and red sandstone; it used to need blaze rods). Hatching Braziers can also be crafted at the Workshop. In Creative, an egg can be set in a brazier anywhere.
- Six wonders stand waiting across the realm, each a whole place on its own site with a keeper: the Great Lighthouse (a 107-block Pharos on the Saltreach cliffs), the High Watch (a 121-block watchtower in a walled bailey), Rimeforge Hall (a great Norse forge hall 75 long with towering forge stacks, a walled forge court, workshops and a stave bell tower), the Sun Gate Caravanserai (a walled desert caravanserai with a great arched gate, minarets and a domed hall), the Lantern Spire (a lantern tower on piles in the fen) and the Great Horse Fair (a tourney arena with stands, gate towers, the royal box and stables). Before the first stage the crew levels the ground under it and grades it back into the land around it. A City can claim one and raise it in six paid stages; finished, it gives its holder, their allies and every settlement in its region a lasting gift (better trade prices, a cheaper Smith, cheaper track and trains, cheaper grooms, a tougher keep heart, or more newcomers). Rival Lords race for them, and anyone with a City can take one by challenge: cut down its guards within ten minutes. The minimap names each wonder as you near it, and its Almanac page opens once your settlement grows. Inside a finished wonder there's more to do: lost travellers come in to the Lantern Spire by its light each dawn and anyone its gift reaches can take them on for nothing (soldiers to your Barracks, settlers to a free bed), and the Great Horse Fair runs timed races round its track (par time, a Fair record and a purse) and jousts against the Fair's champion.
- Rival Lords' towns have townsfolk: the lord's builders put beds in the homes they finish, and newcomers move in at dawn by the same rules as yours. They talk the way their lord rules, hide in their beds while you besiege the lord, and stay on (wary at first) when you take the town.
- Town events: every few days something happens in a settlement, yours or a lord's: a market fair with traders from another region, travelling players on the square at dusk, a wedding, a traveller who asks to stay, harvest day, a cart of spare timber, and now and then something odder. Never on a siege night or the day after, and never the same thing twice running.
- Settler requests: now and then a settler asks for something (a well, another home, hay, lanterns, a bed for their sister). Do it within 7 days for Renown and sometimes coins. Buildings going up sometimes turn something up too, like a pot of old coins in the footings.
- Three story places now have the rooms their books talk about. Crownhold's Hall of Bells has a ruined royal wing: the King's bedchamber, with a rotted four-poster bed, a cold hearth and the chair beside it, and the nursery where the royal children slept, with three cradles. Corvin's Tower has a hearth on its ground floor with a hidden room behind it, under a grassy mound at the foot of the wall, and a cellar under the floor where he was held. The Ashen Gate chapterhouse has a chapel to the east with pews and an altar under a grey window, and the Grandmaster's study upstairs. The Dusty Tomes these books say lie there are now there: in the cradle, by the King's bed, behind the hearth, in the cell, on the Grandmaster's desk and on the reading-room table. The chapterhouse's shelves and lecterns no longer float a block off the floor.
- The Cinder Roost is now a great castle-school spread over the whole mesa above Sunscar: the riders' hall, the domed school wing joined to it by a covered bridge, a cloistered court of the fallen where Kesh keeps the names, the rookery tower, the dragon stables with a landing deck on the roof, a sand training ring, a library, terrace houses stepping down the cliff, and a curtain wall with a south gate. The Span and the perch are as they were.
- The Cinder Roost is alive: wild dragons circle the mesa, dragons stand in the stable arches, sun themselves on the landing deck and sleep by the walls, a cadet flies training circles over the sand ring, and eight riders, cadets, a stablehand, the librarian, the gatewarden and the rookery keeper go about the school and each have five things to say. The climb up from Sunscar is a proper stair of three flights with landings and walls, and the mesa has ledges, gullies and slopes of fallen rock instead of straight walls. The Roost and Crownhold are lit at night.
- Crownhold is now a ruined royal castle on its hill: the Hall of Bells is far bigger, with aisles, empty bell frames, fallen bells and the broken bell tower's top lying in the ward; round it stand the royal wing, a long gallery where every portrait of the last King has been cut out of its frame, a chapel, the keep, the kitchens, round towers and a great gatehouse, with a roofless town running down the slope to a market square.
- Every plot has its own mine: a shaft with a headframe and a ladder, out past the wall, down to a big cave you can explore. Dig anything you like down there. Each night, while nobody is inside, the cave puts itself back the way it was, with the ore in new places: about 12 iron a night at a Hamlet. Deeper levels sit behind cave-ins that come down as your settlement grows, with more iron each tier, gold from City and diamonds at Castle. At Castle, the deepest level has a sealed door at its far end.
- Real caves, made the way vanilla Minecraft makes them: big caverns with stone pillars, long winding tunnels, thin noodle passages, ravines (some open to the sky), underground lakes, lava at the bottom, lush caves under the Vale and Blackfen, dripstone caves under the Emberwaste, Saltreach and Frostmarch, glow lichen, and big copper and iron veins. Cave mouths open on hillsides in open country, never in towns, plots, roads, rail, water, boss sites or the thin places. Every plot has a cave mouth on a nearby hillside, and Kingsrest has two, with iron on the tunnel walls. Ore sits at vanilla's heights. (Needs the next map rebuild.)
- The castle gate and portcullis make real sounds now: the gate creaks open and thuds shut, the portcullis winches up and slams down like a dropped anvil. The Rift's opening (with the rock cracking apart) and the first glimpse of the Hollow have their new recordings too.
- The boss sites are part of the land from the very first day: the Millbrook well and its clocktower, the Antler Crown over Hallowmere, the Drowned Bell Tower and chapel in Gull Bay, the Faceless Gate south of Sunscar, the Cradle Tree south of Mirelight and the Mirelight lantern mast all stand on the map before anyone goes near, so you can see them from far off. Their keepers, villagers and signs still appear when you first come close, and the fights work as before.
- Tilly speaks her greetings now, like the other story characters, and the Wyrm has a new roar.
- A new settlement's first days are now playable from nothing (new-player audit): your house has a founder's chest with logs and saplings of your region's wood, a stone pickaxe and axe, bread, torches and 48 Regals, and a Siege Workshop stands ready in the Mason's Yard.
- The quest tracker's top line now walks you to your first siege: get a pickaxe, open the Workshop, build a Barracks, train guards, hold the night, with the days left until it comes. Founding your settlement lists the same steps in chat, and the day before each of your first three siege nights you are warned.
- Sneak-click a Siege Workshop for its book: the main things it makes by category, what each does and what it takes. A plain click still opens the crafting grid.
- When your settlement grows a tier you are told which town hall you can raise next, where and what it costs. The first time you place a ballista, trebuchet or Battering Ram you get one line on how to work it.
- Woodcutters sell saplings (oak, spruce, birch, acacia, dark oak) and Smiths sell shields.
- Stations at the end of a line (Oakmere, Wolfcairn and Frosthelm) now end the track at a timber buffer beam with a lantern on it and a low stone wall behind, instead of rails stopping in the grass. Stations already standing get it when the settlement next grows (Mateo's town simulator screenshot).
- New players start dressed in their region's working clothes, with its tool for a weapon: a straw hat, jerkin and billhook in the Vale, a fur-trimmed hood, quilted coat and hatchet in Frostmarch, a knit cap, striped smock and cutlass in Saltreach, a sun wrap, sand tunic and sickle-sword in the Emberwaste, an oilskin hat, coat, waders and reed spear in Blackfen (Kingsrest starts get the Vale's). They are given once, when you choose your start. The clothes protect like leather and the weapon hits like a wooden sword; each region's Hunter sells spare clothes and its Smith the weapon, for a few Regals.
- Buildings now settle into the land instead of standing on stone towers: towns, stations and every building you raise with a Foundation Stone cut a level pad into slopes, blend the ground round it in a gentle slope, and build a low retaining wall only where the hill is too steep.
- Town streets now run right out to the edge of the town's ward, along the map road, so they meet the country roads instead of stopping in the grass (Mateo's Kingsrest playtest).
- Gardens, yards and paddocks follow the land: a building still stands on the highest ground under its walls, but its open ground steps down to the street beside it (the capital hall's garden stood a block above the street).
- The forty Faceless statues of King Oswin are now carved 3D figures instead of block shapes: robed and hooded, faceless, with the same pale hands on every one. They line Sunscar's road to the Faceless Gate in pairs, from a boy to an old king, crowned from the young king on, holding a staff, a sword or a bowl or with hands folded, and some broken. The Gate's eight are taller, and four of them have fallen. They stand on their stone plinths when you come near, and nothing can knock them over. (Needs the next map restamp of Sunscar and the Faceless Gate. Until then the old block statues stay where they are.)
- Windmills are built in their region's own materials now, not Vale timber everywhere: dark stone and spruce in Frostmarch, pale stone and prismarine in Saltreach, sandstone with a red cap in the Emberwaste, mud brick and mangrove in Blackfen (Mateo's Sunscar screenshot).
- Every town has a Breeder's Post now, as the guide promised: a fenced breeder's yard in the region's style with the post at its gate, selling that region's foal (Vale Courser in Kingsrest and Millbrook, Frostmarch Destrier in Rimehold, Saltreach Runner in Gull's Rest, Emberwaste Sandstrider in Sunscar, Blackfen Mire-Pony in Mirelight).
- Towns are lit: lamps round every square and along every street, in each region's style (Mateo's playtest: the towns were too dark at night).
- Every building is lit at night now, not just the streets: lanterns hang in every room (on chains in tall halls, on wall brackets along the walls), a lantern hangs beside every door, and courts, yards and decks get bracket lanterns or a lamp post. That covers town halls, every building you raise, stations, trade outposts, mason's yards, the story places, the boss sites (fight floors stay clear, lanterns hang high), the Hollow's sites and arenas in soul light, and vaults. Story places and Hollow sites already standing are built again with their lights; the towns need the next map restamp, and buildings you have already raised get their lights when they next grow.
- The town well no longer stands a block too high: its rim replaces the square's half-slab paving instead of sitting on it.
- Sunscar is a walled desert fortress town: a wall with towers (the east tower among them), a fortress library, the old cistern, a caravan watering place, and a wide avenue south from the square to the Faceless Gate, lined with statues. Gull's Rest gets a chapel with a lectern and a fishmonger's loft; Millbrook gets an Order waystation.
- New story places in the region towns: the net sheds on Rimehold's lake side, the warden's office in Sunscar and a bakery on the Millbrook square. Millbrook's chapel now stands on the square itself.
- Kingsrest and Gull's Rest have proper town walls with a gatehouse on every road out, built in the same stone as their train stations and topped with the station's spired towers. Every street runs out through its gate to the edge of town, and the railway runs outside the wall to the station, which stands in the wall line.
- Eating heals you at once, the way it does in Java Edition: on a full hunger bar, a meal gives half a heart back every half second until it is used up (a steak is about four hearts in four seconds). The slow healing after that is unchanged.
- The story characters speak: Sister Wren, Sir Aldous, Old Mag, the Lighthouse Keeper, Archivist Pell, Brother Cael, Sigrun, Warden Isolde, Nessa, Jorund, Hesper, the Voice in the Water, the Carter and the four mentors now say their greeting out loud when you talk to them, each in a voice and accent of their own (soft rural in the Vale, Yorkshire in the Frostmarch, Cornish in Saltreach, East Anglian in the Blackfen, Welsh in the Emberwaste). Only you hear it, from where they stand. The words on screen are the same hand-written lines.
- Morvane's tome, The Last Testament, now tells the same story as his voice and the last fight: he sent the five bosses to kill you, and when they failed, he let you take their shards and talked you into opening the Rift for him.
- The role mentors have started to notice a pattern. Ulla thinks the Huntsman was sent, Holt says somebody put the Bride across the harbour channel on purpose, Kesh has one more line from Ysolt, Aldous says somebody keeps sending these things at you, and Mag and Nell both warn you about a voice in the dark that speaks kindly.
- The seven thin places are now whole places on the map: a long barrow in a wet ditch with standing stones, a mine escarpment with spoil heaps and a casting pit, an ice-veined gorge, a chalk headland with a flooded sinkhole and a stair cut down the cliff, a flooded hollow with a boardwalk, a salt pan with crystal columns, and a stepped quarry pit with a flooded end. Each cave is built into that land. (Needs the next full map build; older worlds keep the old outcrops.)
- Upkeep: each dawn you are in the world for, your settlement pays for itself and the screen says so ("Upkeep paid: 46 Regals"). It is a sum for the town's size (nothing for a Hamlet, 8 for a Town, 24 for a City, 48 for a Castle), 2 Regals for every building and 3 for every guard. A new settlement pays nothing for its first three days, and days you are away are never charged. If you can't pay, what you have is taken, nothing is knocked down, and fallen guards are not retrained until a dawn is paid in full. If another player wears the Crown, a fifth of what you pay goes to them. The Almanac has a page on it.
- A Breeder's Post that stands in a town now sells that region's breed straight away, with no setting up.
- Works plots: the Ironworks is no longer placed by hand. A town laid out with a Works plot keeps that ground for it: use the plot's marker, pick the Ironworks, and it builds itself there in your region's look, fenced at its full size from the first day with trees on the ground it hasn't built on yet. The Bloomery and the Trip-Hammer Forge then rise on the same plot from the Ironworks block in the yard. You choose the plot, never the exact spot. Older towns with no Works plot still place the crafted block as before.
- The Hollow Choir now leaves a confession when its Conductor falls: The Conductor's Last Measure, the eighth of The Fallen. It tells you who gave the Choir its song, without naming him. The Library now counts 75 tomes.
- The Hollow Choir now drops its own relic the first time it falls: The Last Note, a small violet bell. Hold it in your offhand and things from the Hollow near you falter, slowed and weakened (never a boss).
- The Ash Seneschal tells you at the start that he is watching how you fight, and when he falls the faceless statues in the antechamber grind round to face the vault door again.
- After Morvane falls, a line tells you the Deep Door under the Court will open now.
- The whole story can now be played without commands.
