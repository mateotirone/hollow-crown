# Changelog

What changed in each version of The Hollow Crown, newest first. Written for players: say what they will notice, not which files moved.

How versions work is in [`docs/RELEASING.md`](docs/RELEASING.md).

## Unreleased

Everything merged to `main` since the last release goes here. Each pull request that changes what players see adds a line under one of these headings.

### Added
- A bought stretch of line never tears down a building: its bed only digs through ground and trees, and if a building stands in the way the line goes down around it and the buyer is told where.
- The Vault: choose "Vault" in the Town Hall menu, pay, and you get the Vault Key. Use it on the ground on your land where the stairs should start: a staircase is carved down to an entrance hall and a Wooden Strongroom underground, with a 3x3 vault door and the Vault Lock beside it. Click the lock with the key and the door swings open like a gate (click again to shut it). Keys are cut for one vault: you and your allies open yours with any key, anyone else needs a key cut for it. Hold a relic and click a plinth inside to set it floating there; click it to take it back. Click the lock with an iron ingot, then a diamond, a netherite ingot and a Choirsteel ingot to reinforce it: each tier rebuilds the room bigger with a tougher door. Vault Breakers set against the lock now drill through it (only while the owner is online); when the lock gives, the door falls. Cracked locks and doors mend with the vault's material, and a fallen door is rebuilt from the Vault menu. A keeper away a full real day with a relic leaves their vault open to break-ins for two random half-hours a day (announced in game and on Discord), while their walls, vault and Keep stand twice as strong and their guards fight harder; any login starts the day again. Every vault has a Regal Safe (2 wide, 3 high, can't be mined or moved) holding all your Regals; a thief who gets in breaks it open a blow at a time, each blow paying out a share (shown as a number) and the last one the rest. Better vaults take far more blows and pay less each, and a broken safe is announced to the whole server.
- Regals are now one sum per player: no coins in your pockets, no strongbox, nothing to deposit or withdraw. Coins you pick up are counted in at once, shops take from the sum and rewards add to it anywhere. The Keep menu's Regals button shows it. The Moneyer no longer makes change.
- Townsfolk now walk the streets of Kingsrest (20 of them) and of Millbrook, Rimehold, Gull's Rest, Sunscar and Mirelight (10 each). Click one and they stop, turn to you and talk: everyone has five things to say, heard in order, from gossip and jokes to things worth remembering. They go indoors at night and are back at first light.
- Rail gates: where a line passes through a wall, the gate bars or wall across the track open for an oncoming train and shut behind it. Players on foot can't get through.
- The miniature map in the welcome hall now sits on a white cloud with a puffy, cloud-shaped edge instead of a square stone rim, and its Emberwaste has low dunes, banded red mesas, the odd acacia and dead bushes instead of a flat sheet of sand. The plot stones are now carved markers with a glowing cut gem (green free, amber run by a lord, dark taken) on a little plinth of their region's stone, and the Waystone is weathered stone with a pale blue rune climbing it. Stones you can still choose send up a faint green or amber beam so they are easy to spot across the map.
- The Hollow's roads are easier to follow: a dark oak signpost at both ends of every road names where it leads, soul lanterns mark both ends of every road bridge, and a minecart rail runs beside the road from Cartmeet to the Seams, crossing the light river on a trestle (bring a minecart). The rail and bridge lamps appear in parts of the Hollow nobody has visited yet; signposts appear everywhere.
- Rival Lords build their towns the way you do: the same roads, wall rings and plots as a player's town on that map plot, their hall on its cornerstone, and buildings on plots by the same size and charter rules, so a lord's town grows past a tier only once it has its homes and market. Take a lord's plot and the whole town is yours as it stands, with markers on its empty plots.
- Buying a stretch of line now builds its bed as well as the track: a cutting or tunnel through high ground, gravel on an embankment, and a stone bridge with piers over deep ground or water. There are no empty rail beds across the map any more.
- The Hall Map: every town hall has a painted map of the realm. Open it from the hall's Cartographer's Table or "Hall map" in the Town Hall menu. It shows every settlement in its banner colour and who holds it, rival lords, free plots and the towns, plus places your settlement has found (Crownhold, the Order's houses and more). Land nobody from your settlement has walked stays under fog, and your allies' travels count too. Players never show on it, and mystery places are never marked. Tap a mark to read about it. Sneak-click the table for the ordinary cartography table.
- The Town Board: a big notice board just inside the door of every town hall. Click it to open the Town Hall menu (raise the hall, the wall, the Rumor Board, who's online). Halls that already stand get one too.
- Jumping looks like a jump now: you spring up with your arms swinging high, tuck your knees at the top and reach down for the ground, and the pose starts the moment you leave the ground. How high you jump is unchanged.
- Switches: where two bought lines share track and then part, the track there becomes a switch. A train coming off a branch sets it for itself; sneak + click it with an empty hand to choose which way a train heading out will go.
- Trains go four times as fast as a minecart (32 blocks a second flat out), and the free station carriage now runs at minecart speed.
- The world map now has Rimehold, Gull's Rest, Sunscar, Mirelight and Millbrook built: streets, houses, fields, wells, lamps and trees, with the land graded to the streets. The minimap shows them.
- Every settlement gets its station when it claims its land: a Halt rises on its plot's stop on the planned railway, with no line yet. Buy the first stretch to start travelling. Once a stretch is laid, the station calls a free, slow carriage that drives itself along the line. Engines and coaches can also be bought at any station, and once you've owned one (built or bought) any station gives you a new one free.
- The Looking Glass characters now bend at the elbows and knees (and their armour bends with them): knees bend when you run, jump, land and sneak, elbows bend when you sprint and through every swing, and the drawing hand folds back on a bow. Everyone also picks up new small moves: standing still a while makes you look around, shift your weight or stretch (or check your blade), torches and lanterns are held up ahead, books are held open to read, hands go to the reins when riding, and you flinch when hit. Players on their own skin get all the moves, with straight limbs.
- Walls now carry their own weight. Knock a hole in a wall and the stone above it falls away in a stepped arch; knock out enough and whole sections come down, falling block by block and tipping toward the open side, then shatter into cobblestone and gravel that slides into a slope. Cracked blocks with a lot of wall on top can crush. Reinforced walls reach further over a gap and carry more. Roofs and overhangs you built stay up as long as the wall they hang from stands. Nothing falls in a settlement whose owner is away. Same rules as the Siege Wall Lab.
- Vault blocks (in the Creative menu; how a vault is added, opened, upgraded and broken into comes next): the Strongroom, a vault room in five tiers that grows with each one (wooden 7x7 inside, iron 9x9, diamond 11x11, netherite 13x13, Choirsteel 15x15), each styled in its material, with a staircase down, a 3x3 door with a locking wheel, the Vault Lock in the wall beside it, Relic Coffers along the walls and floating stands for the Crown, the Heart of the Hollow and the Choirsteel template. Also the Vault Key, iron-banded Strongroom Stone, and the attacker's Vault Breaker auger in iron, diamond and netherite (crafted at the Siege Workshop).
- Discord alerts, ready for when the group sets up a Discord: siege nights, fallen Keeps, new settlements, Rival Lords marching, the throne changing hands and more can be posted to a Discord channel, with a ping for whoever it hits, even when nobody is online. A Choirsteel template holder who stays away a full day is announced to everyone. Needs a relay account and a small program on an always-on computer (docs/DISCORD.md); without them nothing changes in game.

### Fixed
- The Hollow's ruler no longer shows a health bar while sitting and talking. The bar appears when you strike them and the fight to overthrow them begins.
- Trebuchet: the boulder now flies with real gravity and can't slip through a thin wall. Look where you want to fire; look higher to lob it, level for a flat fast throw. Sneak + click changes the counterweight (Light, Medium, Heavy) instead of the range. Faster landings hit harder. Only the owner and their allies can fire it, and a hit on someone's settlement makes you Wanted there.
- Battering Ram: it now belongs to whoever placed it, and sneak + click with an empty hand packs it back into an item.
- Boss bars show the boss's name again. The new custom bars left the title blank.
- Gates and portcullises: clicking one always opens or shuts it, whatever you are holding, and never places a block against it. A Castle Gate now swings open like a door: all its joined blocks turn together as one leaf on a hinge at one end, away from you (or the other way if something is in the way), and swing back when shut. An open Portcullis rises out of sight so only its top piece shows, and you can walk under it.
- The Wound landing's dock now reaches the river: a walkway steps down the bank to a landing stage on the water, so you can bring a boat alongside. The crane and a lamp stand out at the end of the stage.
- A dropped Hollow Crown no longer vanishes. Knocked off a head, dropped by someone it killed, or thrown down, it now comes to rest on a small floating stone stand where it fell, slowly turning. The stand can't be hurt, burnt or moved, and the Crown stays there until someone clicks the stand to take it. Dropped over lava or into the void, it goes back to its chest at the Seat instead, so the one Crown can never be lost for good. The Almanac has a page on it.
- The Hollow is a vast dark cave now: a rough rock roof hangs 30 to 50 blocks overhead with domes, hanging rock, amethyst drips, curtains of glowing Veilmoss and great basalt pillars, so there is no sky and no sun. Daylight never reaches it; the land is lit faintly, and by what glows. The fog is a light haze far off instead of a wall 76 blocks away, so you can see as far as your render distance allows. The ground mixes Veilmoss, a new Deep Veilmoss, mycelium, sculk, mud, tuff, blackstone, gravel and more in patches instead of one block. The old leaf-column trees are gone: the Hollow grows its own species from the tree update (Latticewood, Veilmoss Trees, Ashen Deadwood), more of them in the groves. Parts of the Hollow you already explored are rebuilt the new way when you next go near (anything built there is lost).
- The Seneschal's Greatshield is now held like a real shield: it sits on your arm in your off hand like a vanilla shield, and raises when you sneak to brace. The Widow's Veil is thin lace that hugs your head instead of a thick block.
- The Warlord King sits astride his ogre's shoulders instead of standing in the air above its head, and his greatsword is a sensible size.
- The Clockwork Reeve now really throws his cogs: a spinning gear flies from his chest to the marked spot, for both the cog attack and the rewind. Bosses now chase and swing at players in Creative too (Minecraft's own AI ignores Creative players, so they used to just wander), and each hit says how hard it would have landed.
- Boss health bars show the boss's name instead of "Unknown", and each boss now has its own bar: its own colours, frame and title colour (the Clockwork Reeve in brass, the Drowned Bride in sea green, Morvane in purple and magenta, and so on). Other bosses keep the normal bar.
- Track: diagonal pieces no longer lie across the line, bends turn the right way, and slopes climb toward the higher track instead of away from it. Track already laid fixes itself.

### Added
- When your town hall grows to its fifth level it needs more ground: a building standing there comes down with its full price back, and your next building on a plot of the same size is free. That plot is gone for good.
- Settlers move into your settlement. Each needs a bed in a home: at dawn, if there is a free bed, a worker arrives for each finished work building (Smith, Groom, Trader and more), then settlers for the homes. They stand about by day, go to bed at dusk, and say a line or two when you talk to them.
- Built something yourself on a town plot? Use its Plot Marker and pick "I built here myself": if it has what the building needs (a home needs a bed and a door under a roof, a smithy an anvil and a furnace, and so on) it counts like a bought one.
- Guard posts: click one of your guards and pick Station. Your view lifts high over your settlement and every post shows on the ground as a numbered ring, red when empty and green when manned. Pick the wall or a building, then a post, and the guard goes there (two guards swap if the post was taken). Every building has posts by the door and round its corners, and the wall has them all the way round and either side of the gate. Archers hold their post, and Men-at-Arms guard theirs and only chase a short way. The Barracks has a Guard posts button to check them and send a free guard to an empty one. Guards on the wall keep their post when the wall moves out.
- Guards walk to their posts now, up flights of steps on the inside of your wall (every wall you can stand on has four, and towers have a ladder). They keep watch from dusk till dawn and rest by the Barracks by day. When a siege starts, or a Wanted player is on your land, every stationed guard runs for their post at once, and a guard who meets an enemy on the way stops to fight it, then carries on. A guard who can't find a way, or is far away when a siege starts, is sent straight to the post. The Palisade and the Fieldstone Wall now have a walkway behind them, so guards can see over. Walls you already built get their steps the next time the world loads.
- Guards move like soldiers now: a marching walk, breathing and looking round while on watch, a raised sword and a wind-up and diagonal cut for Men-at-Arms, a side-on draw for Archers, a flinch when hit, and a salute when they take a post.
- Sorcerer powers have a proper way to cast now: use the Bound Ember and your hotbar swaps for a magic hotbar with one icon per power you know. Scroll to a power and use it to cast; each icon greys over while it cools down. Use the Ember again (or sneak and use any power) and your own hotbar comes back exactly as it was, even after a death or leaving the world. Every power also has its own casting move (a thrown bolt, crossed arms flung wide for Ward, a bell-ringer's swing for Choir's Call, hands tearing the air open for Veilstep, and more), its own purple and magenta light instead of the plain Minecraft particles, and its own layered sound. The Ember smoulders in your hand, more as your Resonance climbs. The Almanac explains it.
- Dragons now keep the old fear the Cinder Roost talks about: on a siege night no dragon will fly from 03:00 to 03:30, around the 3:17 toll. One that is up glides down, and none will take off. A Dragonlord's (top-rank Dragon Rider's) dragon flies through it anyway, every siege night. That is the Unflown Night, and the first time each night, every raider in the siege host glows for 30 seconds. Kesh tells you about it when you reach Dragonlord.
- Guild Factors in every town: three orders a day for goods from other regions, paid in iron, gold and sometimes diamonds.
- The Ironworks: a settlement iron farm in three stages. The Ore Pit digs raw iron, the Bloomery (Town) smelts it while you feed it charcoal, and the Trip-Hammer Forge (City, beside water) makes about 100 ingots a day. Raiders can loot its storeroom.
- Old Workings: old mines just outside five settlements whose ore grows back (iron at Frosthelm and Gullcliff, gold at Redmesa and Dunehold, diamond in the Wolfcairn Deeps). One runs rich each week, and the deeper ones draw claim jumpers.
- The Moneyer trades metal up a step: 4 iron for a gold ingot, 8 gold for a diamond, 6 diamonds for a netherite scrap.
- Town plots now suit their buildings: each plot lists only buildings of its size, a watchtower needs a plot by the wall, good spots are marked, and a settlement must have its charter standing (homes, a market, later a barracks and more) before sieges can grow it past Town. `/scriptevent siege:town charter` shows what is missing.
- A Plot Marker no longer lands on the road or the door path when its building is finished.
- Waking the Clockwork Reeve. Millbrook's square now has its six stopped clocks on posts, each with a keeper. Ask the keepers which clock the Reeve stopped first: all six answer and one of them lies, so only one clock fits (and which one changes from world to world). Open that clock and its key is still inside: the Stopped Clock Key. Open the wrong one and something climbs out of the well. Wind the key in the well's crank and watch: the clocks tick to 3:18 for the first time in three hundred years, the well drains, and a spiral stair opens down to the clockwork chamber where he waits. The key stays in the crank, so once a siege night has passed after he falls, anyone can wind it again for a rematch. The Stopped Clock Key can no longer be crafted, and using it anywhere but the well does nothing.
- Waking the other four regional bosses, each in its own place. Every key is earned in its region and goes back where it belongs, and none of them can be crafted any more:
  - The Pale Huntsman: Old Sigga, the lodge warden on Hallowmere's south shore, knows the old hunting rhyme. After dark, wake the four carved cairns on the shore in the rhyme's order (the wrong one brings rimewolves) and the last gives the Pale Horn. On a siege night, lay it on the Hunter's Cairn out on the frozen lake: it sounds by itself, something answers, the ice cracks in a ring and he rides up out of it.
  - The Drowned Bride: the Chapel of Saint Brannoc lies under Gull Bay with only its tower above the sea. On a dark-moon night the sea draws back and you can climb down. Lift the floor stone the Lighthouse Letters describe and her ring is under it (lift the wrong one and the drowned climb out). Lay it on the altar: bells under the sea, the pews fill, and she stands up.
  - The Ash Seneschal: a stair at the south edge of Sunscar goes down to five faceless statues of one king, each with a line about when it was carved. Turn them to the vault door in the order of his life (a wrong one sets ash scarabs loose) and a niche gives the Faceless Seal. Press it into the door: the statues turn to look at you and the vault grinds open.
  - The Mother of Changelings: one of the five villagers round Mirelight's lantern square is not who they say. Talk to each, spot the one with all three tells, and say "You're not ..." to their face: what's left is the Mire Effigy (accuse the wrong one and changelings come). Lay it in the root cradle under the Cradle Tree south of Mirelight: a lullaby with nobody singing it, the roots unwind, and she comes down.
  Each key stays in its lock, so once a siege night has passed after the boss falls, anyone can wake it again for a rematch (the Huntsman only on a siege night, the Bride only on a dark moon).
- Each of the five rebuilt Hollow sites now has something to find, once for every player: a boat never launched at the Wound landing, the Woodcutter's Axe in the Lattice Wood lodge, pilgrims' coins and a lantern at Pilgrim's Cross, an unchalked load of Choir Ore in the Cartmeet tally-house, and the Seamcutter pick in the Seams winch house.
- Five places in the Hollow have been rebuilt properly. The Wound landing, where the Carter waits, now has a timber landing stage with a crane and a boatwright's shed with a boat half built. The Lattice Wood is a woodcutters' camp with a log lodge, a sawpit and drying racks. Pilgrim's Cross has a candlelit wayside shrine and a well. Cartmeet has a cart shed and a tally-house. The Seams is a ragged ore cliff with a framed mine mouth and a winch house. Worlds that already built the old versions rebuild them when you next go near.
- Dragon Nests are real nests now: 9x9 blocks of woven branches sunk into the Emberwaste ground, a rim two to three blocks high with twigs flaring off the top, a straw floor scattered with bones, ribs, shed scales and the odd skull, and three big dark red eggs in the middle. Click the eggs to take one; the clutch shows broken shell until the nest lays again. Nests can't be broken, crafted or placed: they only turn up in the wild (a Creative copy grows a whole nest for testing). Old single-block nests grow into whole nests the next time someone is near.
- Every mob and boss moves like itself now. Soldiers march, brutes and the Warlord King lumber, hunters stalk with their heads turning, the Lost Villagers and Changelings shamble with a crooked head that jerks now and then, the Clockwork Reeve moves in ticks, ghosts drift. They lean in when they run, swing whatever they hold (sword slash, axe chop, two-handed cleave, spear thrust, staff cast, claws, bites), archers draw and aim while they have a target, shields come up, and everything flinches when hit. Bosses wind up every attack while the warning is on screen (the Reeve raises both arms to strike the hour, the Huntsman draws high, the Bride throws her head back to wail, Morvane lifts his blade) and release when it lands, and rear up when a new phase begins.
- Players move with more life: sprinting leans in and pumps the arms, you brace in the air and dip when you land from a fall, swords swing forehand, backhand then thrust, axes and the warhammer chop and sweep, bare hands jab and hook, and drawing a bow turns you side-on, shakes after a long hold and follows through when you let go. Works in first and third person. Mining swings are unchanged, and the Rimefang Bow now aims like a bow when other players watch you draw it.
- The welcome hall is now the whole map in miniature: Aldmere at 1 block for every 25, with its coast, mountains, frozen rivers, lakes, fen, desert, roads, towns and forests, floating over Gull Bay. You arrive on a little quay beside the Waystone, and each plot has a glowing stone on its real spot (green free, yellow run by a lord, dark taken). Click one to read about it and start there. Worlds with the old round hall get the miniature in its place by themselves.
- Creative menu for playtests: a Boss Summon for every boss (the five regional bosses, the Warlord King, the Choir, Morvane and a Pretender) in its own "Hollow Crown: Boss Summons" group in the Equipment tab. Use one and the boss appears in front of you with its full fight (the Choir's takes you to the Court first). The guards' own armour, the locked world-tree blocks, the Throne, the Fallen Oath Shard, the Tree of Oaths, the Hollow Crown and the Heart of the Hollow are in the creative menu too, so everything in the add-on can be picked from creative. Every mob and boss already had a spawn egg.
- Riding a train puts you in third person so you can see the line ahead, and gives you your own view back when you get off.
- The Settler's Almanac: every new player gets a small book that explains how things work in Aldmere, chapter by chapter (first days, siege nights, your settlement, coin, travel, growing stronger, books, the wild). The first time you meet something (raise your banner, pick up a Regal, click the Mason, carry a Halter, see a Seep...) you get one line about it in chat, and its page is marked with a gold dot until you read it. Pages about things you haven't come across yet stay hidden, and things meant to be found aren't in it. Type `/siege:guide` to open it without the book, craft another from a book and a feather, or turn the chat tips off on its first page.
- Every role's story now runs to the end. At the fourth rank each mentor tells you what their tradition has always known and never said: Ysolt's last page about the night the grey army went, the bell moulds the Order hid, the back pages of the Debt Book, the old water in the fen. At the top rank each gives you your part in the last fight, and after Morvane falls each has a last scene. A new Knight-Commander names the Order, or keeps the old name.
- Each top-rank role helps at one stage of the Morvane fight: the Forgemaster's bell breaks the Standing Ranks' step, a Knight-Commander's knights hold the wall in the Night It Ended, the Captain's forty row you down the River, a Dragonlord on the wing burns him at the Rift, the Fen Mother's fen slows your fall, and a Sorcerer's blows land harder at the Seat. Alone, the mentors' students stand in (all but the Sorcerer).
- The Cinder Roost has a roll of the fallen: every rider whose dragon dies is added, and Kesh reads it to you. Three cadets go through the trial beside you, and not all of them come back. When your dragon's bond fills, she gives you two points for the Wing branch.
- Four new signature sounds with stand-ins until the recordings exist: the bell-tongue, the roll drum, the forty rowing and the fen collecting (prompts in SOUND-LIST.md).
- The four regional roles have their own stories now. Each has a mentor who finds you before you can earn it: Kesh Cinder at the Cinder Roost above Sunscar (Dragon Rider: cross the Span over the gorge, walk calmly into the nesting field, then hatch your egg), Ulla Deepforge at the Deepforge above Rimehold (Forgemaster: haul iron and coal for her side-hearth, ring Hroda's Hand-bell on the ice at night, bring down the Pale Huntsman), Harbourmistress Holt at Gull's Rest (Captain: pay your share of Holt's Debt, raise a crew of three, lay the Drowned Bride to rest) and Nell Sedge past Mirelight (Witch: drink the cup, kneel at Goody Sedge's three graves, end the Mother of Changelings). The role is offered at the Tree of Oaths when the trial is done; a boss felled early still counts. The Tree has a "Role trials" page that shows your steps.
- Before you choose at the Ember, the Tree now lists the four regional roles and says plainly that taking the Ember's road closes them. Keeping the Ember and deciding later is fine. If a regional deed happens after you already hold a role, you are told why nothing was offered.
- Your mentor speaks to you every time you rise a rank, in all six roles. Old Mag sits you down when you become a Listener and again as an Adept. A Knight is asked by Sir Aldous to stand on someone else's besieged wall (or hold their own as if it were someone else's, when alone), after which the last knights of the Ashen Gate come to them one by one until all eleven stand together.
- After Morvane falls, the story goes on. The five houses of the Hollow that sat at the old truce table all want his empty seat: each sends envoys up on third nights to ask for your backing, every Pretender now fights for one of them, and the house that holds the seat decides how whoever wears the Hollow Crown rules (backers of that house mine Choir ore freely). The keep menu shows "The Five Chairs". And people the Hollow took long ago start coming home, one every week or so, each with their own story and one thing they need before they can rest.
- The seven thin places are real caves now. Each one builds itself the first time someone walks near it, with a Rift seam in the back wall and Brother Fennick's camp outside, where that site's Field Note lies in the tent. Each cave really shows the six things Fennick wrote about (water to swim through, a cracked bell, cut stone, ice and snow, salt crust, hanging roots) only where they are true, so the notes can be solved by walking the caves. After your third regional boss the seven places go into your Ledger as leads, with a rough idea of where each lies; after your fifth you are told how to make the Tuning Fork.
- The hidden third ending. Solve every mystery in the Ledger, and once Morvane has fallen, say the land's old name at the empty Seat. The Choir answers and the old truce holds again. From then on every third night is a Guest Night: people of the Choir come up and stand by every keep, the Even Market opens at Reedmoor's ring of posts (a thing for a thing, no coin), nobody is taken, and nothing in the Hollow hunts you. Ring a bell at Bellhollow and the truce breaks: every third night the Hollow comes for your walls again, harder than it ever did, until someone makes the truce again.
- The Hollow has a story between the Rift and the Choir. A nameless cart driver, the Carter, meets you at the Wound landing and walks the stretch with you. The Truce Hall, the Standing Ranks, the Far Door, the top of the Drift and the Seams each play a short scene the first time you reach them, and the Seams asks you to choose whether to wreck the dawn ore cart. See any three of those five and the Carter teaches you the Cartsong. Without it the Court will not let you in. Two stretches of the path have new names: the Landing Reach and the Truce Country.
- The story opening works on the real map with no commands: the first time the map loads it places the twelve Plot Stones, builds the welcome hall in the sky and puts Sister Wren on a chapel porch on the Millbrook square (a stand-in until the town is built). New players wait a moment at Kingsrest, go up to the hall, choose a plot, get the journal and their plot's first book, and can walk to Millbrook to find Wren. Starting in Kingsrest without a plot now gets you pointed to Wren too.
- Everything that opens a menu when you click it now has floating text over it while you are close: the Keep Banner, Station Post, Rail Depot, Stables, Foundation Stone, Plot Stone, Hall Cornerstone, Tree of Oaths and the rest show their name and what clicking does, and traders, the Mason, lords and story folk show "Click to trade" or "Click to talk" above their name. `/scriptevent siege:labels off` hides them all.
- The Forester's Lodge (Siege Workshop: three logs, two iron axes, an oak sapling, three planks): place it on your land and a fenced grove of your region's trees goes up behind it. Chop a grown tree at the bottom of its trunk and it comes down, dropping its logs at the stump; a sapling takes its place and is full grown again in three days. Click the Lodge to choose what each plot grows and to open more plots (2, 4, then 6).
- Hand-built trees for every region: 31 real species turned into Minecraft-style trees (great oaks and weeping willows in the Vale, tall tiered spruces in Frostmarch, cypresses and olives in Saltreach, knee-rooted bald cypresses in Blackfen, palms and baobabs in the Emberwaste, Latticewood in the Hollow), each in four sizes with bark-coloured stone walls, stair and trapdoor branches, fence twigs and hanging leaves. The trees the map places cannot be mined, burned or blown up for now.
- The Hollow's mining villages, the sites at its centre and the drowned city are rebuilt in the Hollow's own crooked style: leaning timber houses, a fallen Truce Hall, and seven leaning towers on the marsh banks by the Court landing. They build a block at a time, so the world never stalls. Worlds that already built them keep the old look.
