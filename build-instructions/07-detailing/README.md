# Stage 7: Detailing

The building stands and is closed. Now the outside is finished: depth with slabs and stairs, fences,
arches, lights, greenery, signs, and the edge where the building meets the street. Detailing makes a
build look lived in. It is also where most clutter comes from, so every detail has a reason.

## Principles

- **Every detail answers "why is this here?"** A bracket holds something, a lantern lights a door, a
  barrel stands by a shop, ivy grows on the shady side. Details with no reason are clutter.
- **Detail clusters at the human level and at the edges**: doors, windows, corners, the eaves, the plinth.
  Big plain areas between are fine; they let the details read.
- **Symmetry on formal fronts, balance on homes.**
- **Less is more at doorways** (the Kingsrest lesson).

## Depth with slabs and stairs

Where to add, all one block proud of the face unless said otherwise:

| Detail | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| String course | every floor line on stone buildings; the plinth top | a row of upside-down stairs facing out, or top slabs, full length of the face, wrapping corners with outer-corner stairs |
| Drip course | top of a 2-plus plinth | upside-down stairs facing out |
| Cornice | under the eaves on stone and civic buildings | 1 or 2 rows: upside-down stairs, then top slabs above them |
| Pilasters | between bays on civic fronts | a column of a contrasting block, 1 proud, the full storey, with a stair cap and base |
| Buttresses | stone walls over 9 tall or 15 long, every 5 to 7 | 1 to 2 proud at the bottom, stepping in with a stair at mid height and at the top |
| Corbels / brackets | under jetties, balconies, oriels, bay windows, turrets, eave overhangs of 2 | upside-down stairs, tall side to the wall |
| Window caps and sills | every window (stage 5) | |
| Beam ends | proud-frame timber builds at the floor lines | log ends (axis out of the wall) between posts, every other block or under each post |
| Round angles | any inside corner that should read as a curve: opening tops, under jetties and eaves, coved ceilings | trapdoor, stair, trapdoor (`reference/shapes.md`, "Round angle") |
| Ledges under gables | gable bottom on stone buildings | a row of top slabs |

Half-block rule: use **slabs** where you want a thin line (half a block of depth, a thin cap), **stairs**
where you want a shape (a bracket, a curve, a step), **trapdoors** where you want the thinnest edge (a
flat frame, a shutter, a lip on a planter) and **walls / fences** where you want a round post.

## Arches (outside the openings too)

- Arches between buildings over an alley, carriage arches through a range, arcades on market halls and
  Saltreach civic fronts: see `reference/shapes.md` for every width.
- An arch over an alley (Mateo's alley reference, `reference/pictures/stone-alley-arches.png`) springs
  from the wall of one building to the other at upper floor level, stone, with a hanging lantern on a
  chain in the centre and vines growing over it.
- Arcades: a row of 3-wide arches on piers 1 wide, odd number of arches, the centre arch on the
  building's centre line.

## Fences and rails

| Use | Rule |
|---|---|
| Balcony and gallery rails | fence of the trim wood on the edge, a post at each end and at each post of the frame |
| Stair rails | along the open side of a flight, outside and in |
| Posts for awnings, stalls, signs, lantern posts | fence posts on a full block base (a log or stone block in the ground) |
| Garden and yard enclosures | fence or walls with a gate centred on the path; posts every 3 or 5 |
| Window boxes and bars | fences are not bars: use iron bars in stone windows, fences only in timber |
| Never | fences as the only support of a floor or a stair roof (`02-frame`); fences on a roof edge as decoration |

## Lights

- A lantern beside or over every main door; hanging lanterns under jetties and canopies; lantern posts at
  steps and gates; lanterns hung from chains under arches.
- Lights at regular rhythm on long fronts: every 5 or 7 (odd), on the bay posts.
- The night should feel uneasy, not black: warm lights on the human scale, no floodlit faces.
- Hide light blocks for interiors under carpets or behind trapdoors in empty buildings.

## Hanging and spiked details (Mateo, 2026-11-11, `reference/pictures/detailed-hall-hanging-chains.png`)

Layered, dense detail for halls, inns, towers and grand houses (keep cottages simpler, detail balances
detail: `texturing/`).

- **Chains**: lanterns hang on chains (1 to 4 links) from every verge tip, eave corner, jetty corner and
  bracket end. Long chains from the highest corners, short under porches. Odd spacing, symmetric pairs.
- **Fences as hangers and posts**: a fence post sticking down under a beam end or bracket, with a lantern
  under it; fence posts standing on parapets and balcony corners; never fences as structure (`02-frame`).
- **Ridge spikes and finials**: a spike every 3 or 5 along the ridge (end rods, or a wall block or fence post
  with a lightning rod on top), taller finials at the gable peaks and tower tops (`06-roof/roof-types.md`).
- **Layers**: every horizontal line gets two or three layers stacked: beam, then a row of upside-down
  stairs, then trapdoors or slabs under it. Every post gets a base and a capital of stairs or trapdoors.
- **Pillars with trapdoors**: logs and stone posts dressed with trapdoors open against their faces, so a
  1x1 post reads thicker and carved.
- **Greenery hanging**: leaves and vines hanging off balconies and floor bands, between the lanterns.
- **Accent band**: one thin band of an accent colour (oxidised copper, prismarine) along a floor line or
  balcony rail on grand buildings, the same accent as the town's civic roofs.
- Look at the picture closely: it reads as rich because every edge has a second and third layer, and
  lights hang everywhere at the same small scale, not because it uses many different blocks.

## Greenery

Mateo's cottage sheet and alley picture are full of green; it is what makes them warm.

- Vines and glow lichen on the shady side, at corners and over arches; never covering a whole face.
- Leaves as bushes at the plinth (1 to 2 blocks), flower beds along the front path, berry bushes and
  flowers in planters.
- A tree near most houses in timber regions (Claude generates the trees, see the map rules); azalea and
  flowering azalea for colour.
- Moss: only as a light touch in the bottom courses of old stone walls, and on Blackfen roofs. Large green
  patches on a wall read as grass.
- Region greenery: Vale flowers (poppy, cornflower, dandelion, azure bluet, oxeye daisy, tulips);
  Frostmarch spruce, ferns, snow; Saltreach dried kelp, sea pickles in pots, sand-coloured flowers;
  Blackfen moss, mangrove roots, lily pads; Emberwaste dead bush, cactus in pots, no grass; Hollow glow
  lichen, Veilcap and Glimbulb.

## Signs, props and life

- Shops: a hanging sign or banner on a bracket by the door showing what they sell; barrels, crates,
  sacks (bundled hay, composters) by the door.
- Homes: a bench (stairs with trapdoor arms) by the door, a woodpile (stripped logs) on a side wall, a
  bucket or cauldron by the back door.
- Awnings: striped wool and carpet on fence posts over shop windows (Emberwaste and market stalls), in
  the region's awning colours (`settle_buildings.LOOK`).
- Keep 1 to 3 props per door. More is clutter.

## The street edge

- Path from the street to the steps, the width of the steps.
- A 1-block apron of path or gravel along the front, grass or garden at the sides.
- Corner houses get detail on both street faces.

## Before you move on

- [ ] At least three depth layers on every face over 5 long.
- [ ] String courses, cornices, buttresses where the table says; corners wrapped.
- [ ] Every fence has a job; rails on every balcony, gallery and open stair.
- [ ] Every main door lit; lights in an odd rhythm on long fronts.
- [ ] Greenery on the shady side and at the ground, not covering faces; no large green patches on walls.
- [ ] 1 to 3 props per door; nothing blocks a door, window or path.
